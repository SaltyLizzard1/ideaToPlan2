## 9. 90-Day Action Plan

The stage and current state of QYLAT were not captured in the intake, so this plan is written for a business that has not yet made its first paid consulting sale. If you already have paying clients, skip the validation phase and start from the next unanswered question: what is the repeatable path to the next client?

Before any paid delivery, complete one action to check registration, licensing, tax registration, and insurance requirements. That check is built into the plan below.

The targets in this roadmap are IdeaToPlan planning thresholds. They are not industry benchmarks or researched indicators of viability.

| Days | Phase | Goal | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days 1-14 | VALIDATE | Confirm that your target customer has the problem you describe and is willing to pay to solve it | 10 conversations held; at least 2 people express willingness to pay $500 for a planning session |
| Days 15-30 | REFINE | Sharpen the offer based on what you heard and make the first sale | 1 paying client; offer description revised once based on conversation findings |
| Days 31-60 | ACQUIRE | Build a repeatable path to new clients through direct outreach and early content | 2 paying clients in this period; at least 1 referral conversation initiated |
| Days 61-90 | MEASURE AND EXPAND | Assess what is working, identify the highest-converting activity, and decide whether to hold or push toward Base | 3 paying clients in this period; conversion rate tracked across all outreach |

---

**Days 1-14: VALIDATE**

**Action 1: Write down the specific experience behind your differentiator.**

Your stated differentiator is "experience." That is not yet specific enough to use in a conversation or a positioning statement. Before you talk to anyone, write two or three sentences that name the concrete experience, results, or capability you bring: what you have done, where you have lived or traveled, what decisions you have navigated, and what that means for a client. This is not marketing copy; it is the raw material you will test in conversations.

- Why it matters: A vague differentiator will not hold up when a prospect asks why they should choose you over a free resource or a cheaper alternative. You need a specific answer before the first conversation.
- Done when: You have written a two-to-three sentence description of your specific background that a stranger could read and understand without follow-up questions.
- Metric: One written description, completed before the first conversation.

---

**Action 2: Identify 10 people in your target group and hold a conversation with each.**

Your target customer is an adult, typically 40 to 60, who is financially stable and considering a major lifestyle change but does not know where to start. You do not need a large audience to do this. Start with people you already know or can reach directly: former colleagues, friends, community members, or people in relevant online groups who fit the description.

The goal of each conversation is not to sell. It is to hear, in their words, what is keeping them stuck, what they have already tried, and whether they would pay for a structured planning session. Ask: "If someone could sit with you for an hour and give you a clear, personalized plan for making this change, what would that be worth to you?"

The founder's hypothesis is that the primary barriers are money, logistics, career concerns, and fear of making the wrong decision. These conversations will test that hypothesis.

- Why it matters: The $500 price, the offer format, and the problem description are all untested assumptions. Ten conversations are the fastest way to find out whether any of them are right before you spend time or money building anything.
- Done when: 10 conversations completed and notes taken on: the problem each person described, what they have already tried, and their reaction to the $500 price.
- Metric: 10 conversations; at least 2 people say they would pay $500 or ask how to book.

IdeaToPlan test criterion: if fewer than 2 of 10 people express willingness to pay $500, do not proceed to selling at that price. Revisit the price, the problem framing, or the target customer before continuing.

---

**Action 3: Check registration, licensing, tax registration, and insurance requirements.**

This must be completed before your first paid delivery. The model flags business registration (planning assumption: roughly $200 if required) and professional liability insurance (amount not yet established) as conditional costs. Because QYLAT will offer personalized financial and life-planning advice to clients globally, the regulatory picture is not straightforward. The intake does not confirm whether a legal entity exists.

- Why it matters: Delivering paid consulting without the required registrations or insurance creates legal and financial exposure. This is a check, not an assumption: pay only if it turns out to be required.
- Done when: You have spoken with an accountant or business adviser in your jurisdiction and have a written answer to: (1) whether you need to register a business entity, (2) whether professional liability insurance is required or advisable, (3) what your tax reporting obligations are for income from international clients.
- Metric: Written notes from the conversation; a decision made on each of the three questions above.

Note: the $300 accountant or business adviser consultation is budgeted as a one-time cost in Months 1-3, after the first sale. If you need this answer before you can make a first sale, treat it as a pre-sale cost and fund it from your startup budget.

---

**Days 15-30: REFINE**

**Action 4: Revise the offer description based on what you heard.**

After 10 conversations, you will know which problem framing landed, which words your target customers used, and what they said they wanted as an outcome. Rewrite your offer description using their language, not yours. The offer as modeled is: a personalized lifestyle-change planning session in which the client receives a clear, actionable plan for transitioning to long-term travel, relocation abroad, or a more flexible lifestyle, including identification of their key barriers and concrete next steps. That is a planning assumption for the format; confirm or adjust it based on what you heard.

- Why it matters: An offer described in the customer's own words converts better than one described in the founder's words. This is a hypothesis worth testing in the next round of outreach.
- Done when: You have a revised one-paragraph offer description that uses at least three phrases or words you heard directly from conversation participants.
- Metric: One revised offer description, completed before the first outreach attempt.

---

**Action 5: Make the first sale.**

Go back to the people from your 10 conversations who expressed interest. Send a direct, personal message: explain what the session includes, what they will walk away with, and how to book. Do not build a website or a booking page first. Use a simple calendar link (free tools exist for this) and collect payment manually or through a basic payment link. Choose your payment processor before this step and confirm its per-transaction fee, because that cost applies on every sale and is not yet in the model.

- Why it matters: The first sale is the only test that matters at this stage. Everything before it is a hypothesis.
- Done when: One client has paid and a session is scheduled.
- Metric: 1 paying client; payment received before the session.

>> FIRST REVENUE MILESTONE: 1 paying client at $500. This is the signal to move from validation to acquisition.

---

**Action 6: Deliver the first session and document what you did.**

Run the session. Immediately after, write down: how long it took including preparation and follow-up, what the client said they valued most, what felt unclear or incomplete, and whether the two-hour delivery assumption held. The model assumes roughly two hours per session including preparation and follow-up notes; this is untested.

- Why it matters: The financial model's capacity and profit figures depend on the two-hour assumption. If sessions take four hours, the Stretch scenario becomes physically difficult. You need this number before you commit to a volume target.
- Done when: Notes completed within 24 hours of the session, covering time spent and client feedback.
- Metric: Actual hours logged; one piece of client feedback captured.

---

**Days 31-60: ACQUIRE**

**Action 7: Set up a scheduling and invoicing tool.**

Now that you have a paying client, set up a simple scheduling and invoicing tool ($15 per month, budgeted after first sale). This removes the manual back-and-forth from booking and payment and lets you focus outreach time on finding the next client rather than administering the last one.

- Why it matters: At two or more clients per month, manual scheduling creates friction that costs you time and makes the business look less professional to prospects.
- Done when: A booking link is live and a test invoice has been sent and paid successfully.
- Metric: One complete booking-to-payment cycle completed through the tool.

---

**Action 8: Run a second round of direct outreach, targeting 20 to 30 people.**

The Base scenario assumes the founder can consistently reach roughly 30 warm prospects per month through direct outreach alone; this is untested. In this period, test whether that volume is achievable. Identify 20 to 30 people who fit your target customer description and reach out personally. This can include people from your existing network, referrals from your first client, and relevant online communities where your target customer is active.

Where to look is a hypothesis at this stage: no verified evidence was found about where adults aged 40 to 60 considering major lifestyle changes look for help online. IdeaToPlan recommends starting with communities you already have access to, because that is the fastest and lowest-cost test. If you have an existing audience on any platform, test whether they convert before building a new channel.

- Why it matters: The model's Base scenario requires 30 leads per month at a 10% conversion rate. This round tells you whether that volume is reachable through direct outreach and what conversion rate you are actually getting.
- Done when: 20 to 30 outreach messages sent; responses and outcomes logged.
- Metric: Number of conversations started; number of bookings made; conversion rate calculated.

IdeaToPlan test criterion: if fewer than 2 bookings result from 20 to 30 outreach attempts, the conversion rate or the outreach channel needs to change before you scale volume.

---

**Action 9: Ask your first client for a referral and a testimonial.**

After delivering the first session, ask directly: "Do you know anyone else who is thinking about making a similar change?" and "Would you be willing to share a few sentences about what the session gave you?" A referral conversation and a written testimonial are both assets that cost nothing to acquire and have direct value for the next sale.

- Why it matters: The Target scenario assumes that early client referrals expand the prospect pool. This is untested. The only way to find out whether referrals will materialize is to ask.
- Done when: You have asked the question and recorded the answer, whether yes or no.
- Metric: 1 referral conversation initiated or 1 testimonial received.

---

**Days 61-90: MEASURE AND EXPAND**

**Action 10: Track your numbers and calculate your actual conversion rate.**

By Day 61 you have run at least one round of outreach, delivered at least one session, and have data on how long sessions take, how many conversations lead to a booking, and where your leads are coming from. Calculate: total outreach attempts, conversations started, bookings made, sessions delivered, and hours spent. Compare these to the model's assumptions.

- Why it matters: The model assumes 10% conversion from lead to client. If your actual rate is 5%, you need 60 outreach attempts per month to reach Base, not 30. If it is 20%, you need only 15. This number changes everything downstream.
- Done when: A simple log exists with the four figures above, updated after every outreach round.
- Metric: Conversion rate calculated; actual hours per session confirmed.

---

**Action 11: Publish your first piece of content tied to a specific customer problem.**

The QYLAT model depends on content to build awareness and trust over time, eventually reducing the outreach burden. IdeaToPlan recommends starting content only after the first sale, so you know which problem framing resonates. Write or record one piece of content, on whichever platform you already use or have access to, that addresses one specific barrier your conversation participants named. Do not build a website yet; the model places the website build after traction (3 paying clients per month), which the forecast does not reach until Months 7-9.

- Why it matters: Content that addresses a specific, named problem your target customer has expressed is more likely to attract the right audience than general travel inspiration. This is a hypothesis worth testing.
- Done when: One piece of content published; the specific problem it addresses is written down so you can track whether it generates inbound interest.
- Metric: 1 piece published; any inbound inquiries from it logged separately from outreach-driven inquiries.

---

**Action 12: Decide whether to hold at current volume or push toward Base.**

By Day 90 you have data on conversion rate, session delivery time, client feedback, and whether referrals are materializing. Use that data to make one decision: hold at current volume and refine the offer further, or increase outreach volume to push toward the Base scenario of 3 clients per month. The forecast reaches Base in Months 7-9. If your conversion rate and delivery time support it, you can reach Base earlier; if they do not, the forecast gives you time to adjust.

- Why it matters: Scaling outreach before the offer is working wastes time. Holding too long after it is working delays revenue. This decision requires your actual numbers, not the model's assumptions.
- Done when: A written decision made, with the reason, based on the data from the previous 90 days.
- Metric: Decision documented; next 30-day outreach target set.

>> DO THIS FIRST: Before any outreach or selling, write down the specific experience behind your differentiator in two to three sentences. Every conversation you hold in Days 1-14 depends on having a clear, honest answer to "why you?"

>> BIGGEST RISK: The $500 price and the problem framing are both untested. If 10 conversations produce fewer than 2 expressions of willingness to pay at that price, the model's revenue figures do not hold. Run the conversations before building anything else.
