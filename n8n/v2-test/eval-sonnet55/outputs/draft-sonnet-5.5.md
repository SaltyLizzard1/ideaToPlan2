## 9. 90-Day Action Plan

The intake did not give a business stage, current revenue, or what you already have in place (audience, website, tools, past conversations). This plan is written for an offer that has not yet had a paid session, as a working assumption. If you already have paying customers, an audience, or completed customer conversations, use what you learned, start from the next unanswered question in the phase below, and raise the targets. The thresholds in this section (conversation counts, booking counts, deadlines) are IdeaToPlan planning thresholds. They are not industry benchmarks and do not show that the business is viable.

| Days | Phase | Goal | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-14 | VALIDATE | Learn from people in your target group what stops them, what they have tried, and whether they would pay for a personal planning session | 10 conversations held. At least 2 of the 10 say they would book a paid session |
| 15-30 | REFINE | Turn the conversations into one written offer, settle the legal and payment checks, and make the first sale | 1 paying customer ($500 at the assumed price) |
| 31-60 | ACQUIRE | Find customers beyond your first conversations and set up the tools the Budget places after the first sale | 1 more paying customer (2 in total) |
| 61-90 | MEASURE AND EXPAND | Compare actual results with the forecast and decide what to keep, change, or stop | 1 more paying customer (3 in total) and a written Day 90 review |

The targets stay within the forecast for Months 1-3 of 1 customer per month, $1,500 revenue over the three months, $90 operating expenses, and $300 in one-time costs. The $500 price is an untested scenario assumption, not a price you chose. Spending before the first sale is $0, apart from the payment processor fee, whose amount is not established. The Budget table in section 7 sets the stage for each cost.

>> DO THIS FIRST: Spend one hour writing down the specific experience behind your offer, then message ten people in your target group this week and ask each for a conversation.

### Days 1-14: VALIDATE

**Action 1: Write down what "experience" means in specifics.**
- Why it matters: The intake gives your differentiator as "Experience" with no detail. That is not yet specific enough to build positioning on, and the conversations will test whether buyers value it.
- Done when: You have one page listing the countries or lifestyle changes you have personally handled, the results, and the problems you could solve for a customer. It ends with two sentences that say who you help and with what.

**Action 2: Hold ten conversations with people aged roughly 40 to 60 who are considering a major lifestyle change.**
- Why it matters: Your description of customers' barriers (money, work, logistics, fear of the wrong decision, not knowing where to begin) is a hypothesis. These conversations are the cheapest way to test it.
- How: Start with warm contacts. If you already have an audience or email list, invite people from it too. The size of any audience was not captured, so how well that route works is unknown.
- Ask what is stopping them, what they have already tried, which of the five barriers weighs most for them, what outcome they would want from a session, and whether they would book one at $500 or what they would pay instead. Free alternatives exist, such as the free expat financial consultations offered by Expat.com with Holborn Assets [S4] and by Creative Planning International [S11]. Ask whether they have used anything like these.
- Done when: Ten conversations are logged with notes. Each log says whether the person named a specific barrier, what they had tried, and what they said to the price question.
- Metric: IdeaToPlan test criterion: at least 2 of 10 say they would book a paid session. If fewer than 2 do, change the price, the offer, or the target group before Days 15-30, and run ten more conversations.

>> VALIDATE THIS: At least 2 of your first 10 conversations should end with a person saying they would book a paid session. This threshold is IdeaToPlan's, not a market benchmark.

### Days 15-30: REFINE

**Action 3: Write the offer on one page.**
- Why it matters: The model's primary offer is a personalized planning session that leaves the customer with a clear plan, their key barriers identified, and concrete next steps. The intake does not give a session length or format.
- Proposed assumption for you to confirm: one planning session plus a written summary of the plan and next steps. The model assumes about 2 hours of your time per session, including preparation and follow-up. This is untested, so record the real time on the first sessions.
- Done when: The page states who it is for, what the customer receives, the price (start from the $500 assumption and adjust only on what the conversations showed), and how to book.

**Action 4: Check whether registration, licensing, tax registration, and insurance are required, before you ask anyone to pay.**
- Why it matters: The intake does not say where you are based or whether a legal entity exists, and none of this was established by research. The Budget carries $200 for business registration if required and professional liability insurance with no amount established, both conditional.
- How: Start with the free official guidance for your location. Pay for registration or insurance only if it turns out to be required. If you remain unsure, the accountant or adviser consultation ($300, an assumption) is the Budget's after-first-sale item. It can happen after the first customer has paid and before you deliver that session.
- Done when: You have a short written note for each of the four items saying required, not required, or still unsure, with where the answer came from and the cost if required.

**Action 5: Choose a payment processor and confirm its per-transaction fee before the first sale.**
- Why it matters: A fee applies on every sale, but its amount is not established and is in none of the profit figures. Confirming the rate is how that cost stops being unknown.
- Done when: You have a processor selected, you can take a payment, and the fee rate is written next to your price.

**Action 6: Offer the session to the people from Action 2 who said yes, and deliver the first paid session.**
- Why it matters: A stated willingness to book is not payment. A paid session is the first test of the price.
- How: For the first session, use a video tool you already have. The paid scheduling and video tools wait until after the first sale.
- Done when: One customer has paid and had a session, and you have recorded the time it took and what they received.
- Metric: 1 paying customer by Day 30, which is the forecast's Month 1.

>> FIRST REVENUE MILESTONE: One paying customer, which is $500 at the assumed price, by Day 30.

### Days 31-60: ACQUIRE

**Action 7: Set up the tools the Budget places after the first sale, if you do not already have them.**
- Why it matters: The Budget places a scheduling and invoicing tool ($15 per month) and a paid video conferencing tool ($15 per month) after the first sale. These are assumed rates, not quotes. Use any equivalent you already pay for instead of buying again.
- Done when: A customer can book, pay, and join a session without manual back-and-forth.

**Action 8: Hold the accountant or business adviser consultation.**
- Why it matters: The Budget carries $300 (an assumption) after the first sale to confirm tax and reporting obligations for a business that operates "everywhere".
- Done when: You have written answers on your tax and reporting obligations, and on Action 4's items if they were still open.

**Action 9: Debrief your first customer.**
- Why it matters: This is your first paid evidence on whether the outcome was worth the price and whether it is worth repeating.
- Done when: You have asked what they got from the session, what was missing, and whether they would recommend it. You have also asked permission to quote them, and have noted any introductions they offer.

**Action 10: Hold ten new conversations with people outside your first ten, and offer paid sessions to those who say yes.**
- Why it matters: The first customer likely came from your warm contacts. Whether people outside that circle buy is a separate, untested question. The model's Base scenario assumes direct outreach alone can supply the leads.
- How: If you already have a free assessment or planning tool, use it as the entry route and record how many people who finish it ask for a conversation. If you have no such tool yet, use a short written questionnaire sent by message or email, which costs nothing. No paid advertising is in the model, so none is scheduled here.
- Done when: Ten new conversations are logged with the source of each lead.
- Metric: IdeaToPlan test criterion: 1 more paying customer by Day 60 (2 in total). If none by Day 60, review price and offer against the conversation notes before doing more outreach.

### Days 61-90: MEASURE AND EXPAND

**Action 11: Ask each paying customer for a referral or a testimonial.**
- Why it matters: The Target scenario assumes referrals expand the prospect pool, and this is the first test of that assumption.
- Done when: Each customer has been asked, and you have counted how many introductions or quotes resulted.

**Action 12: Run another round of outreach and sell the third session.**
- Why it matters: The forecast reaches 3 customers over Months 1-3, so this tests whether the pace holds.
- Done when: You have held another ten conversations and made offers.
- Metric: 1 more paying customer in Days 61-90 (3 in total, $1,500 revenue, equal to the forecast for Months 1-3).

**Action 13: Write a one-page Day 90 review.**
- Why it matters: This turns three months of activity into a decision.
- Done when: The page lists these figures:
  - conversations held, offers made, and sessions sold
  - revenue received
  - actual hours per session against the 2-hour assumption
  - what customers said about the price
  - which source produced each paying customer
  - the processor fee you actually paid per sale
  - any legal or insurance cost that turned out to apply
- It ends with one decision: keep the offer and price, change them, or change the target group.

**Do not buy the "after traction" items yet.** The email marketing tool ($20 per month), the website build or template ($500), and website hosting ($20 per month) are placed after traction. The model assumes that point is 3 paying customers per month, and that is an untested assumption. If you already have a website or an email tool, use it instead of buying a new one. The Day 90 review is the place to decide whether the results justify these purchases earlier or later.
